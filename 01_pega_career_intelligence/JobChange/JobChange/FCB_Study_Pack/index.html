How to use this pack

About the worked example. Every chapter in this pack follows one application: Deposit Servicing, built for a fictional US bank called Riverbend. The customers, accounts, balances, case IDs, staff, policies, thresholds, SLAs and architecture are invented for teaching. They are not First Citizens' architecture, data or policy, and nothing here should be read as describing any real bank's internal processes. Where a figure is a legal requirement rather than an invented policy, it is cited to the regulation. Class names keep the FCB-Dep- prefix purely so the rule-resolution examples stay consistent across chapters.

Chapters 01 to 13 are written to be read in order. A customer, Dana Whitfield, asks Riverbend to change her address; the request needs rules to run, a journey to travel, data to act on, a screen to be seen on, a core system to be updated, events and files to keep it current, and eventually controls, a database to report from, a regulatory reason for each rule, and an architecture that holds all of it together. Each chapter picks up the problem the last one ended on, makes the application able to do one more thing, and closes with a short recap of what it can now do and what it still cannot.

The reference tables that used to open every concept are still there, but they now come after the explanation that makes each field meaningful, and the exhaustive ones sit in blocks marked as reference so a first reading can skip them. Chapter 14 is different: it is a revision chapter of short model answers that point back to the chapters for the teaching.

Suggested path. Read 01 to 04 for the platform, 05 to 07 for how the application talks to everything outside it, 08 and 09 for the adjacent capabilities worth understanding even if you have not shipped them, 10 and 11 for operating and querying it, and 12 and 13 for the domain and the architecture. Keep chapter 14 for the last day.

Chapters

Part I — Platform foundation

01 · Pega Platform Core & Rule Resolution Dana's address-change request arrives and must behave correctly for her market and product: rules, classes, inheritance, rulesets and the five-phase rule-resolution walkthrough, then the clipboard, requestors, locking, guardrails, testing and packaging. 02 · Case Management, BPM, SLAs, Routing, Correspondence The same request travels through intake, review, approval and fulfilment: stages, assignments, routing, a rejection and a late document, urgency and SLAs, how the SLA engine actually fires, correspondence. 03 · Data Model, Data Pages, Declaratives, Decisioning The case needs account data it does not own: one lookup through a data page, scope and refresh, writing back through a savable page, exposed columns and the BLOB, declaratives, decision rules, circumstancing, validation. 04 · UI: Traditional, Constellation, DX API Marcus opens the case, edits a field and submits it: traditional UI and Constellation as two separate implementation paths, and the DX API carrying the same change from the web channel.

Part II — Integration and event processing

05 · Integration & Authentication Reading Dana's account from the core and asking it to change: mapping, authentication, then a slow core, a rejecting core and a core that loses its response, and what timeouts, idempotency and retries must do about each. 06 · Kafka, Data Flows, Queue Processors, Job Schedulers Balances that are a day old, and one balance event followed from topic to acknowledgement; duplicates, ordering, a crash, a backlog and a replay. 07 · File Listener / SFTP / Parse Rules, Email Listener / Channel A partner that can only send files, and a request that arrives by email: readiness, parsing, per-row isolation, duplicate protection and reconciliation.

Part III — Adjacent capability

08 · Pega AI, GenAI, Knowledge Buddy, Agentic AI, CDH Finding the right procedure and classifying documents: deterministic rules, predictive models, retrieval and agents as different tools, with a bank's governance questions attached to each. 09 · Pega RPA & Intelligent Automation One legacy screen with no API: how a queued work item reaches a robot and returns, why bots break, and how to argue for replacing one.

Part IV — Engineering hygiene

10 · Security, Okta/SAML, Splunk, PDC, CI/CD Marcus tries to approve his own change: access control, segregation of duties, deployment and an incident worked from symptom to prevention. 11 · PostgreSQL & SQL for Pega Engineers A business question about the deposits data, answered with progressively harder queries, concurrency on Dana's row and one execution plan.

Part V — Domain, architecture and revision

12 · Deposit Operations Domain The account lifecycle first, then a payment, a hold, a dispute and a closure as separate journeys, each with its regulation, clock and evidence. 13 · FCB Target Architecture & Case Blueprints The application revisited as a whole: one request through every layer, then scale, failure, deployment and recovery. 14 · Master Interview Question Bank Understanding turned into answers: the direct answer, the mechanism, one example, two likely follow-ups and the common mistake — plus the design scenarios, the first-90-days plan and the résumé mapping.

The honesty rule.
