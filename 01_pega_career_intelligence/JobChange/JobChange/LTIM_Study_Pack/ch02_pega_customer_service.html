2.1 What Pega Customer Service is

Plain-English definition

Pega Customer Service is a strategic application: a pre-built, industry-oriented product that sits on top of the Pega Infinity platform. It gives a contact centre or service operation a working agent desktop, a customer-interaction model, a library of ready-made service processes, and hooks for channels and decisioning. Chapter 01 introduced the platform-versus-strategic-application distinction; this chapter depends on it.

The sentence to hold on to is: it is not a platform feature and it is not something you obtain by building cases yourself. You can build customer-service-style case types on bare Infinity. That produces a customer-service application. It does not produce Pega Customer Service, any more than building your own spreadsheets produces a particular accounting package.

2.2 Why it exists, and the metrics that justify every decision

The problem it solves

A customer calls with a billing question, then asks to change a payment date, then reports a lost card. From the customer's point of view that is one conversation. From a typical back office it is three tickets in three systems, none of which knows about the others, and an agent switching between screens while the customer waits. Before purpose-built service applications, organisations stitched this together with a CRM front end, a case tool and a knowledge base. Each seam caused cost: re-keying data, repeated authentication, lost context on transfer, and no consistent record of what happened.

Pega Customer Service addresses the seam. It treats the conversation as the unit that the agent works inside, and the individual pieces of work (change a date, report a card) as service requests that run inside it with the same case engine, SLAs, routing and audit that any Pega case has.

The metrics you must speak

Pega Customer Service design decisions are justified in contact-centre terms. You will be expected to use this language. Four metrics dominate, with one more worth knowing.

MetricDefinitionHow it is usually computedWhat moves it
AHT (Average Handle Time)The average time an agent spends handling a contact, from start of talk through hold and any after-contact wrap-up work(Talk time + hold time + after-contact work time) divided by the number of contacts handledContext pre-loaded on screen, fewer system switches, guided steps, suggested replies, automated wrap-up notes
FCR (First Contact Resolution)The share of enquiries fully resolved on the first contact, with no repeat contact for the same issueResolved-first-time contacts divided by total contacts, over a defined repeat windowGiving agents the authority and data to finish the job; fewer hand-offs; accurate knowledge
NPS (Net Promoter Score)A loyalty measure from asking customers how likely they are to recommend the company on a 0 to 10 scalePercentage of promoters (9 to 10) minus percentage of detractors (0 to 6)Overall experience over time; a consequence of the metrics above rather than a direct lever
ContainmentThe share of contacts fully handled in self-service or automation without reaching a live agentContacts resolved in self-service divided by all contacts that began in self-serviceQuality of self-service journeys, digital messaging, and how well the bot or portal hands over when it cannot finish
CES (Customer Effort Score), worth knowingHow much effort the customer had to expend to resolve the issueSurvey-based; Pega Customer Service includes a post-conversation survey service request type of this kindFewer steps, no repeated information, quick resolution
AHT = (talk time + hold time + after-contact work) / contacts handled FCR = contacts resolved first time / total contacts (within a defined repeat-contact window) NPS = % promoters (9-10) - % detractors (0-6) Containment = self-service contacts resolved without an agent / self-service contacts started

A worked example, with illustrative numbers

ILLUSTRATIVE A service centre handles 10,000 contacts a month at an AHT of 8 minutes: 80,000 agent minutes, about 1,333 agent hours. A redesign pre-loads customer and account context so the agent no longer asks for details or opens other systems, and automates the wrap-up note. Suppose that cuts one minute from every contact. AHT becomes 7 minutes; the work is 70,000 minutes, about 1,167 hours. The saving is about 167 hours a month at the same volume.

Now see why FCR matters more than it appears. Suppose 25 percent of contacts are repeats because the first contact did not resolve the issue: 2,500 repeat contacts at 8 minutes is 20,000 agent minutes. Raising FCR so that repeats fall to 15 percent removes 1,000 repeat contacts, 8,000 minutes, and also improves the customer's experience, which NPS later reflects. A trade-off sits inside this: an agent rushed to reduce AHT may leave an issue half-resolved, which lowers FCR and creates repeat contacts that raise total handle time. The metrics must be read together.

THE METRICS AS A SYSTEM better context + guided steps ----> lower AHT -----+ authority + accurate knowledge ---> higher FCR ----+--> fewer repeat contacts --> lower cost good self-service + clean handover -> higher containment | v customer effort falls --> NPS rises over time Caution: pushing AHT down alone can reduce FCR and raise repeat contacts
The honest limitation. You have not operated a contact centre, so do not quote a benchmark AHT or NPS as though you have seen one. Give the definitions and the trade-offs, and say that you would expect any Pega Customer Service design discussion to be anchored to the client's own baseline numbers. If asked for an industry figure, say you would not quote one without a source.
Lead-level framing. "In a contact-centre implementation I would ask for the baseline AHT, FCR and containment before designing anything, because each screen, suggestion and automation is a bet on moving one of them. I would also watch for a design that improves AHT at the cost of FCR, because that just moves effort into repeat contacts."

2.3 Interaction, service requests, case types and Microjourneys

The Interaction as a wrapper case

The central modelling idea in Pega Customer Service is the Interaction. An Interaction represents a conversation or contact with a customer: a phone call, a chat session, an email thread. It is a case in its own right, and it is the wrapper inside which the service work is done. When an agent picks up a call, an Interaction exists. It identifies the customer, holds the context of the conversation and records what happened. The actual work the customer wants done is performed by service requests launched from within the Interaction.

INTERACTION AS WRAPPER Customer contacts you (voice, chat, email, digital message ...) | v INTERACTION (wrapper case: who is the customer, what channel, what is the conversation about) |-- service request: Billing Inquiry -- resolved during the call |-- service request: Change Address -- resolved during the call |-- service request: Report Lost Card (long-running)-- continues in the back office after the call ends | Interaction ends: wrap-up, outcome recorded, history preserved on the customer record

TermWhat it isRelationship to the others

Service request typeIn Customer Service terminology, a case type: the template of a repeatable piece of work with stages and stepsThe Pega documentation states that in the Customer Service industry a case type is called a service request type
Service requestA running instance of a service request type: one customer's billing enquiry, one address changeLaunched inside an Interaction (or created from a back-office channel)
Case typeThe Infinity platform term for the template of a processService request type is the Customer Service name for the same concept
MicrojourneyOne part of an overall customer journey that achieves all or a subset of the required business outcomes; in Pega applications a case type represents the work of a MicrojourneyPlanning concept (a business outcome) mapped to one case type or service request type

Microjourney, as Pega uses the term

The Pega documentation defines a Microjourney as one part of an overall customer journey, for example a banking journey, that achieves all or a subset of the required business outcomes for the application. Its examples: the Make Payment Microjourney is the work needed to collect and apply a customer payment to an account or policy; the Change Address Microjourney is the work needed to update a customer address for an account or policy. In your Pega application a case type represents the work that takes place during a Microjourney. In the Customer Service industry the case type is called a service request type, and an instance is a service request.

Separately, Pega's delivery approach (Pega Express) uses the Microjourney as a planning unit. The documentation lists the key elements of a Microjourney as case types (reusable templates of business processes), personas (the parties that interact with cases through channels) and data objects (the data needed to process a case). So "Microjourney" is both a design concept (one journey slice) and a planning concept (case types plus personas plus data objects plus channels). Interviewers may use either sense; the common ground is that a Microjourney is a bounded business outcome delivered by a case type.

Do not confuse these. Journey (the whole customer experience across time and channels), Microjourney (one bounded slice of it), case type (how Pega implements the slice), and service request (one running instance). Candidates often say "microjourney" to mean any small process. Use it only for a bounded business outcome.

Why the wrapper matters

  • Context travels. Because service requests run inside the Interaction, they inherit the identified customer and the conversational context. The agent does not re-identify the customer for each request.
  • One conversation, one record. Reporting on contacts (volume, duration, outcome) is done on the Interaction; reporting on work (what was requested and resolved) is done on service requests. Those are different questions and the model separates them.
  • Long-running work outlives the call. A service request can continue after the Interaction ends, with its own SLA, routing and back-office handling. That is how the call stays short while the work completes.
  • AHT versus resolution. The Interaction timer measures handle time; the service request SLA measures resolution time. Both exist because both matter.
Lead-level framing. "The Interaction is the wrapper case for the conversation; service requests are the work launched inside it, and each service request type is a case type that implements one Microjourney. The wrapper is what carries the identified customer and context, and it lets the call end while long-running work continues in the back office with its own SLA."

Plain-English definition

The Interaction Portal is the desktop that customer service representatives (CSRs) and back-office workers use to answer and resolve customer enquiries that arrive through channels such as phone, digital messaging and email. The Pega documentation also states that managers use it to resolve escalations, track team work and generate insight reports. It is built so that an agent can serve a customer from a single screen.

A channel is the way a customer reaches the organisation. What integration under a live agent's hands looks like

Every channel adds real-time integration constraints that batch and back-office systems do not have. A CTI event must create the right Interaction before the agent answers. A customer lookup must return within a couple of seconds while someone waits on the phone. A downstream core system that takes ten seconds to respond is invisible in a batch job and unacceptable in a call. This is the part of Customer Service work where your integration experience transfers best: Connect-REST and Connect-SOAP calls with timeouts, data pages with sensible refresh strategies, and fallbacks that let the agent continue when a backing system is slow. Describe it exactly like that, without claiming CTI or messaging set-up experience.

Do not claim channel set-up. You have not configured CTI, a messaging channel or co-browse. Say that you understand how they feed the Interaction model and that your relevant experience is the integration and routing behind a live contact.
Lead-level framing. "Whatever the channel, the model is the same: identify the customer, create or link an Interaction, launch service requests, route and track them. What changes per channel is the real-time integration and the human-factors constraint that someone is waiting. I would design for the slowest dependency first."

How NBA relates to CDH, precisely

Candidates often blur these. Next-Best-Action is the pattern: for a given customer at a given moment, recommend the single action (or a few) with the highest value subject to rules. Customer Decision Hub is the product that implements that pattern at enterprise scale, with arbitration across the organisation's actions and channels. Pega Customer Service has a built-in, simpler, deterministic source of suggestions; when an organisation also licenses CDH, Customer Service can display CDH's recommendations in the same area, and the documentation describes mapping CDH actions to Customer Service case types so that accepting a recommendation launches the right service request.

Not your experience. You have not worked with CDH, NBA strategies, Pega Knowledge or Pega's GenAI features. Describe them as conceptual understanding. For CDH specifically, say you understand the decisioning pattern and that the decisioning implementation would be owned by a decisioning specialist alongside you.
Lead-level framing. "Suggestions are a bet on AHT and FCR. I would start with deterministic, context-driven task suggestions that map to existing service request types, measure acceptance and resolution, and only then layer CDH offers with suppression rules for sensitive contexts. For generative summaries I would settle review, retention and guardrails first, and I would confirm the capability in the deployed release's documentation."

2.8 Extension points

Why extension discipline matters more here

Because Customer Service is a framework, the quality of an implementation is largely determined by how well the team extends it. A team that rewrites framework rules in place creates an upgrade burden on every release; a team that extends through supported mechanisms inherits improvements. This is the strategic-application version of the "build close to out-of-the-box" philosophy in Chapter 01.

Pitfalls

Anti-patterns: editing framework rules in place instead of copying into your own layer; creating a new service request type for something an out-of-the-box one covers; hard-coding contact reasons that do not match the reporting taxonomy; loading every customer composite eagerly on every interaction; building a custom component for a layout the template already supports; wiring suggestions to offers without suppression for complaints.
Lead-level framing. "Extending a strategic application is mostly a discipline of restraint. I would classify every requirement as adopt, adapt or new, keep customisation in my own layer, and record exceptions. Because I have built a reusable framework inside a client before, I know that the cost of a clever extension arrives at upgrade time."

2.9 The honest mapping: what your work transfers and what it does not

The ambiguity, stated plainly

The "Customer Service" line on your resume. Your resume lists Customer Service alongside Collections, Debt Recovery and Remarketing at Mercedes-Benz Financial Services. That is a business function built as custom case management. It is almost certainly not the Pega Customer Service strategic application. A Pega Customer Service interviewer will ask about the Interaction Portal, interaction wrapper cases, service request types and composites within two minutes. If you imply framework experience you do not have, the interview ends there. The accurate and still valuable statement is that you built case management for a customer-service business function using core Infinity.

The exact sentence

Say this, in your own words. "At Mercedes-Benz Financial Services I built case management for customer-service-type work, alongside Collections, Debt Recovery and Remarketing: case types, SLAs, routing, correspondence and the integrations behind them, on core Infinity 8.x. That was custom case management for the business function, not the Pega Customer Service strategic application. I have not implemented the Interaction Portal or the service request framework, and I understand them from the documentation. I would ramp quickly because the case engine underneath is the same."

Why this works: it claims the real credit (case types, SLAs, routing, correspondence, integration), it draws the line before the interviewer does, and it ends on the true bridge (the engine underneath is the same). Say it unprompted if Customer Service comes up. A candidate who draws that line first is trusted for everything else they say.

Never say: "I have worked on Pega Customer Service." "I have built the Interaction Portal." "I have used service request types." "I have configured composites." "I have worked with the CSR assistant." If an interviewer asks "which Customer Service version?" and you answer with a number, you will be asked which composite configuration you changed.

2.11 Interview Q&A (Sathvik's voice)

Model answers are conceptual understanding plus a ramp plan. None claims product experience you do not have.

Q1. What is Pega Customer Service?

"A strategic application built on Infinity: a framework with its own class hierarchy, customer and interaction data model, Interaction Portal and ready-made service request types, plus hooks to channels, knowledge and decisioning. It is not a platform feature, and building customer-service case types yourself does not give you the product. I know it from the documentation rather than from delivery."

Q2. Have you worked on Pega Customer Service?

"Not the product. At Mercedes-Benz Financial Services I built custom case management for customer-service-type work alongside Collections, Debt Recovery and Remarketing on core Infinity 8.x. I own the case engine side: case types, SLAs, routing, correspondence and integration. I have not implemented the Interaction Portal or the service request framework, and I have a plan to explore them in a demo environment."

Q3. What is an Interaction?

"The wrapper case for a customer contact. It identifies the customer, holds the conversation's context and records the outcome. The work the customer wants is done in service requests launched inside it, so context travels, and long-running work can continue in the back office after the call ends."

Q4. What is the difference between an Interaction, a service request type and a case type?

"An Interaction is the wrapper for the conversation. A service request type is the Customer Service name for a case type, the template for a repeatable piece of work; a service request is one running instance of it. So a service request type is a case type, and each represents the work of one Microjourney."

Q5. What is a Microjourney?

"As Pega defines it, one part of an overall customer journey that achieves all or a subset of the required business outcomes, for example Make Payment or Change Address. A case type represents the work in a Microjourney. In Pega's delivery approach it is also a planning unit made of case types, personas, data objects and channels. I use the term only for a bounded business outcome."

Q6. Walk me through the Interaction Portal.

"Pega Academy's Customer Service on Constellation topic says the Interaction Portal consists of six functional areas: the navigation bar with dashboards, notifications, recents, phone and email access; the customer details summary that stays visible; the suggestions area with recommended tasks, offers and articles; the action area where the current work is done; composites summarising customer and account information from systems of record; and utilities and channels such as telephony and messaging. Exact labels vary by release and by UI Kit or Constellation form, so I would confirm them for the deployed version."

Q7. What is the difference between the UI Kit and the Constellation Interaction Portal?

"The UI Kit portal is section-based and server-rendered; the Constellation portal, introduced in Pega Customer Service 23, is view-based and client-rendered on the design system. Customisation differs: header and composite configuration plus sections for UI Kit, views and DX Components for Constellation. They can coexist, with the Constellation portal running service requests built in UI Kit, which lets an organisation migrate service request types gradually. I have not worked on either in production."

Q8. What do AHT, FCR, NPS and containment mean, and how do they affect design?

"AHT is average handle time: talk, hold and after-contact work per contact. FCR is the share resolved on first contact. NPS is promoters minus detractors on a likelihood-to-recommend scale. Containment is the share resolved in self-service without an agent. Each feature is a bet on one of them, for example pre-loaded composites target AHT, authority and knowledge target FCR. The trap is optimising AHT alone, because rushed calls lower FCR and generate repeat contacts that raise total handle time."

Q9. How do suggestions work, and how does NBA relate to CDH?

"Suggestions come from Customer Service's own deterministic logic or from Customer Decision Hub if it is licensed, and are ranked into tasks, offers and articles with a small number shown at a time. Next-Best-Action is the pattern; CDH is the product implementing it at enterprise scale. I understand them conceptually and have not worked on CDH. I would start with deterministic task suggestions, measure acceptance and resolution, and only then add offers with suppression for complaint contexts."

Q10. How would you handle GenAI summarisation in a service implementation?

"It is release-sensitive, so I would first confirm what the deployed release supports. Then I would settle the governance before enabling it: what data goes to the model, whether an agent reviews the summary, where it is stored on the case, and how it is audited. The benefit is lower after-contact work, so it targets AHT. I have not configured Pega's GenAI features."

Q11. How do channels differ, and what is the common model?

"Voice with CTI, email, chat and digital messaging, self-service and co-browse each add their own real-time integration, but they feed the same model: identify the customer, create or link an Interaction, launch service requests, route and track them. Availability and routing decide who receives the contact. I have not configured the channels, but I have built the integration and routing under time pressure that sits behind them."

Q12. How would you extend an out-of-the-box service request type?

"Classify the need as adopt, adapt or new. Adopt as shipped when it fits; copy into my own layer and adapt when it is close; create new only when nothing fits. I would never edit framework rules in place, because that creates an upgrade burden. In a sandbox I would copy a type such as Make Payment, change a step and a form, and note what the framework allows."

Q13. Which Customer Service Microjourneys do you know?

"Communications has Billing Inquiry, Add Mobile Device and Plan, Guided Internet Setup, Make Payment and Move Service. Financial Services has Change PIN, Fee Inquiry, Make Payment, Manage Lost or Stolen Card, Travel Notification, Payment Inquiry, Repossession and Collateral Management and Manage Customer-Circumstance. The last two are near my domain: hardship and arrangements, and vehicle finance. That is domain overlap, not product experience."

Q14. Your resume says Customer Service. What exactly did you build?

"Custom case management for customer-service-type work at Mercedes-Benz Financial Services, alongside Collections, Debt Recovery and Remarketing, on core Infinity 8.x: case types, SLAs, routing, correspondence, and the REST, SOAP, SQL and Kafka integrations behind them, across seven markets. It is not the Pega Customer Service strategic application. I would rather clarify that than let the line mislead."

Q15. What transfers from your collections and debt recovery work?

"Case lifecycle design, SLAs and escalations, routing, correspondence, integration under a live operational load, production support, and a seven-market rollout. What does not transfer is the Interaction model, the portal, the service request framework, contact-centre metrics ownership, channel set-up and CDH. I have mapped both lists and have a plan for the second."

Q16. How would you ramp on Customer Service in the first month?

"Read the Interaction Portal, service request type and Microjourney documentation; complete the Academy Customer Service topics; open a demo environment, start a demo interaction and run Make Payment through its stages; copy one service request type and change a step; and write a one-page comparison against my own case types. By the end I would honestly say I have explored it hands-on in a demo environment, and I would ask the engagement lead which portal form and release the client is on."
