How to use this pack

Each chapter is a standalone HTML file that prints to A4. Every chapter follows the same shape as the FCB Study Pack, so you always know where to look:

Per concept

  • Plain-English definition
  • Why it exists — the problem it solves
  • How it is implemented, with the real names in a table
  • A diagram of the runtime path

Then applied

  • A concrete worked example
  • Pitfalls and anti-patterns
  • Lead-level framing callouts
  • Interview Q&A with model answers in your voice
Companion pack. This pack is a sibling of the FCB Study Pack. Where Chapter 03 builds on traditional UI and DX API groundwork it refers to FCB Chapter 04 rather than repeating it.
The honesty rule this pack is built on. What you have genuinely done — case management, flows, SLAs, routing, Data Pages, Kafka ingestion, Queue Processors, REST and SOAP integration, release management, production support — is written in confident first person. What you have not done — the Pega Customer Service product, the Interaction Portal, Constellation, the DX API, DX Components, the React SDK, React, Pega Blueprint, CDH, and Infinity 24.x — is written as conceptual understanding plus a credible ramp plan, never as experience. Three issues are handled head-on: the “Customer Service” line on your resume (Chapter 02, section 2.9), the Constellation and React gap (Chapter 03), and 8.x against the job description's 24.x (Chapters 01 and 03). A panel of Pega practitioners will find an overclaim in minutes.
How to read the evidence tags. VERIFIED a company fact stated on an LTIMindtree-owned page, a Pega-owned page or a Pega press release; JD-SOURCED what a job posting reproduced by an aggregator advertises, not a description of the whole practice; INFERENCE reasoning from the evidence, never to be stated to an interviewer as fact; CLAIM — NEEDS DETAIL a resume outcome whose method, scope or measurement is not recorded, so be ready to supply it; ILLUSTRATIVE a teaching number, never to be spoken as your own measurement. No LTIMindtree client name, headcount, internal team name or interview-process detail appears anywhere in the pack, because none has been established.

Lead-level framing. Across the three chapters the through-line is the same: lead with what you own (Kafka, Queue Processors, case management, integration, release discipline), name each gap before you are asked, show the bounded scope of the gap, and present a concrete plan with something you can honestly say you have done. Interviewers at a services firm are sizing how fast you become billable on a new release; a credible plan answers that directly.
