What Changed in 2026 — SQL
Interview Intel Report
Refresh window: H2 2025 → H1 2026. Last compiled: May 2026.
This file is the field intel layer of the pack. It is not a tutorial. It is a short, dense readout of what we have
been hearing from candidates and hiring managers over the last twelve months — what is being asked
more, what is being asked less, what is showing up that did not show up a year ago, and what you
should triage if you are interviewing this week.
If you came into this pack worried that SQL prep material gets stale fast, this section is the answer to
that worry. We refresh it every six months. Everything below is keyed to rounds happening between June
2025 and May 2026 in the Indian market.
Executive Summary — the five shifts that matter most
If you only read one page of this file, read this one. These are the five shifts that have most materially
changed the shape of a 2026 SQL interview versus a 2024 one.
Window functions are now a baseline expectation, not a senior topic. ROW_NUMBER, RANK,
LAG, LEAD, and a basic SUM OVER (PARTITION BY …) frame are now appearing in fresher and 0-
to-2-year rounds at both Service MNCs and product cos. A year ago, most freshers could get
through Round 1 without writing one. That window has closed.
Snowflake-specific questions are surging in product co and data role rounds. Clustering keys,
virtual warehouse sizing, time travel, zero-copy cloning, and the materialized view vs dynamic table
tradeoff are now showing up in roughly half of mid-level product co rounds where the stack involves
a warehouse. Eighteen months ago these were specialist questions; now they are general data
engineer screening material.
MERGE INTO is replacing the manual INSERT-or-UPDATE pattern in Postgres conversations.
Postgres 15 made MERGE generally available, and interviewers have caught up. If a candidate
writes a hand-rolled upsert when MERGE would do, it is being flagged as a knowledge gap in
roughly the same way that not knowing JOIN syntax used to be.
Isolation level questions are moving earlier in the interview funnel. Read Committed vs
Repeatable Read vs Serializable — with concrete scenario framing rather than rote definitions — is
now appearing in Round 1 or Round 2 at fintech and payments cos. Previously this was a Round 3
system design adjacent topic.

JSON column patterns have crossed the line from nice-to-know to expected. JSONB in
Postgres, JSON in MySQL 8, and the equivalent in SQL Server 2022 are now interview-table-stakes.

A few framing notes before the body of the report:
These shifts are biased toward the Indian market between June 2025 and May 2026. US-market
data, where we have visibility, mostly agrees on the direction of these shifts but the magnitudes
differ. The Snowflake surge, for example, is roughly six months ahead in the US.
"Service MNC" in our taxonomy means TCS / Infosys / Wipro / Accenture / Cognizant / HCLTech for
the purposes of this report. "Product co" means Indian-headquartered consumer or B2B product
companies. "GCC" means India-based captive centres of US-headquartered firms.
We weight signal by recency. A pattern we last heard about in November 2025 is given less weight
than one we have heard about in March or April 2026. This means the trends in this report skew
toward the most recent six months.
The single biggest meta-shift behind all of these specific shifts is that interviewers have decided
their question banks were stale, and have been actively refreshing them. That refresh started roughly
twelve months ago and is still in motion. Expect another smaller wave of shifts in the next six
months as the refresh propagates further.
How we source this
We track patterns we hear about — nothing more, nothing less. The signal comes from a few places and
we want to be straightforward about what those are so you can calibrate.
The main input is candidate-reported interview content. Candidates who use our prep material tell us
what they were actually asked after their rounds. That stream gives us pattern data — which question
shapes are recurring across companies, which are dying off, which are new. We do not have access to
interviewer panels or hiring rubrics directly. We do not publish question-for-question leaks; we aggregate
the shape of what is being asked.
The second input is hiring manager and senior engineer conversations — people on our network who run
rounds at the companies in this report. They tell us what they have added, dropped, or reweighted in the
last quarter. This is qualitative, not quantitative.

Service-MNC patterns 2026
Service MNCs — TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, HCLTech — run high-volume hiring
processes with relatively standardized question banks. The good news for candidates is that the
question shapes are predictable. The shift over the last twelve months is that the question banks have
been refreshed, and the new patterns are visibly more practical and less rote-definition than what we
tracked in 2024.
TCS
TCS rounds at the 2-to-5 year experience band are now consistently including the following patterns,
where "consistently" means we have heard them in the majority of rounds reported back to us.
⚡ 
Second-highest salary by department using DENSE_RANK. This used to be a "self-join trick"
question; it has migrated to a window function question and the expected solution is now 
DENSE_RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY dept ORDER BY salary DESC). Candidates who
write the old correlated subquery version are not being marked wrong, but they are being asked the
follow-up "can you do it with a window function?" with notable frequency.
Delete duplicates from a table where there is no surrogate key. The expected answer
increasingly involves a CTE with 
ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY <natural key cols> ORDER BY <something>)
and a DELETE on rn > 1. The older GROUP BY + EXISTS pattern is still accepted but considered the
long way around.
Convert rows to columns without using PIVOT. This is becoming a litmus test for whether the
candidate genuinely understands CASE expressions inside aggregates. The framing is usually "pivot
monthly sales into columns" — and the interviewer is checking whether you reach for SUM(CASE
WHEN month = 'Jan' THEN amount END) reflexively.
Find Nth highest salary with a CTE-based approach. TCS has historically loved this question; the
shift in 2026 is that the accepted answer now uses a CTE and window function rather than the older 
LIMIT 1 OFFSET N-1 pattern. Both still work; interviewers are explicitly probing for the CTE-
with-window version.

Date-range gap detection. Given a table of subscription periods, find gaps. This is new in 2026

Infosys
Infosys has historically leaned into procedural SQL more than the others. That is still true in 2026 but the
balance is shifting.
Recursive CTEs for hierarchy traversal. Employee-manager hierarchy queries are now expected
to be written with a recursive CTE. Candidates who only know how to do it with self-joins for two
levels are being correctly identified as candidates who have not written this before.
CASE in ORDER BY. Sort customers so that VIPs appear first, then by last_purchase_date. This is
showing up in roughly a third of mid-level rounds we hear about.
Window functions with PARTITION BY for running totals. Running totals as a windowed SUM
rather than a self-join. The interviewer typically prompts "without using a self-join" if the candidate
reaches for the old pattern.
GROUP BY with HAVING for cohort filtering. "Find customers who placed orders in every month
of Q1 2026." The expected pattern uses HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT month) = 3 and is now
considered a baseline expectation rather than a clever trick.
EXISTS vs IN, with a concrete reasoning question. Infosys has been explicitly asking candidates
to explain when EXISTS is preferable to IN, with a NULL-handling angle. This is new flavour for 2026
rounds.
Wipro
Wipro's mid-level data engineer rounds have absorbed more analytics-engineering content over the last
year.
NTILE for percentile bucketing. Bucket customers into deciles by spend. We did not see this
much before; it is now appearing.
LAG/LEAD for month-over-month comparison. Calculate MoM growth for a metric. The expected
solution uses LAG and a divide; candidates who try to do it with a self-join on month-1 are being
asked to try again with a window function.
CROSS APPLY (where the stack is SQL Server) or LATERAL (where Postgres). Wipro rounds
have started probing whether candidates have ever written a correlated row-by-row computation
using LATERAL. It is not yet a majority-of-rounds question, but it is climbing.
Index design for a given query plan. Show me the indexes you would create to support this query.
This is a shift away from rote "what is a clustered index" definitions and toward applied reasoning.

Accenture
Accenture's pipeline has tilted noticeably toward cloud-warehouse-adjacent SQL.
Snowflake-flavour SQL. Even when the role is not explicitly Snowflake, Accenture interviewers are
increasingly asking questions like "how would this query behave in Snowflake vs Postgres" or "what
is a clustering key." If your CV mentions any data platform, expect a touch of this.
Window functions with multiple PARTITION BY columns. "Rank products within category within
region." The compound PARTITION BY is now an expected baseline.
CTE chains of 3+ levels. Multi-step queries where each CTE feeds the next. The shift here is that
interviewers are evaluating readability of the CTE chain, not just correctness.
Date math without DATEADD/DATEDIFF aliases. Expect questions where the dialect is not
specified and you need to write date arithmetic that translates. This is checking whether you have
written cross-dialect SQL or only one engine's flavour.
Cognizant
Cognizant has historically been the most "interview question bank" of the Service MNCs. The 2026 bank
is broader and trickier.
⚡ 
ROW_NUMBER vs RANK vs DENSE_RANK distinction. Appearing in the vast majority of mid-
level rounds we have heard about. The interviewer typically asks for output for each on the same
input data with ties. Candidates who can recite the definitions but stumble on the example output
are being filtered out.
Self-join for finding pairs. Find all pairs of employees in the same department earning the same
salary. Classic, still being asked, still mostly being botched in the join condition (forgetting the < to
deduplicate pairs).
GROUP BY with multiple aggregates and HAVING. Composite filter logic post-aggregation. This
is being used to test whether candidates conflate WHERE and HAVING.
JSON column query (if Postgres or MySQL 8 is on the resume). Given a JSON column, extract a
nested field and filter. New for 2026 at Cognizant; we did not see it in 2024.
Transactional scenario question. Two transactions, what does each see — typically framed around
bank balance updates. The expected answer requires the candidate to know what isolation level the
engine defaults to.

HCLTech
HCLTech rounds are increasingly leaning into Oracle and SQL Server flavours because of the BFSI client
base.
Oracle-specific MERGE syntax. If the role is BFSI-adjacent, expect MERGE INTO questions in
Oracle syntax even if your background is Postgres.
Analytical functions with KEEP DENSE_RANK FIRST/LAST. Oracle-specific construct. Showing
up in senior rounds.
Window frame clauses — ROWS BETWEEN vs RANGE BETWEEN. Climbing in volume. The
interviewer is checking whether the candidate has actually written running totals over time-series
data and run into the RANGE vs ROWS gotcha.
PIVOT / UNPIVOT in SQL Server. Still being asked, still expected as built-in syntax answer rather
than CASE expression hack at the senior end.
Product-co patterns 2026
Product companies — Razorpay, Swiggy, Cred, Meesho, Zerodha, Flipkart, PhonePe — are where the
SQL interview has shifted the most in the last twelve months. The depth is up across the board. These
rounds are no longer "can you write a JOIN"; they are "can you reason about a query plan, isolation
behaviour, partitioning, and write a non-trivial window function chain."
Razorpay
Razorpay's SQL bar is high, particularly on the payments backend side. The 2026 patterns are
concentrated on transactional correctness.
⚡ 
Idempotent payment write under concurrent retry. Given two concurrent attempts to mark a
payment as captured, what SQL pattern do you use to ensure idempotency. The expected answer
involves a unique constraint plus INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING (Postgres) or an
equivalent. New baseline for 2026.
Isolation level question with read skew scenario. Two queries, one update in between, what does
each query see under Read Committed vs Repeatable Read. Appearing in roughly half of mid-level
Razorpay rounds.
Deadlock reproduction. Given two transactions updating two rows in opposite order, will they
deadlock. This is being asked verbally rather than coded — the interviewer wants to hear you reason
about lock acquisition order.

Postgres 15-16 — what's new and being asked
Postgres 15 and 16 shipped features that did not exist eighteen months ago in any meaningful interview-
prep sense. They now do. Here are the eight Postgres-specific topics that have been climbing.
1. MERGE became generally available in Postgres 15
MERGE landed as a first-class statement. Before this, the idiomatic upsert pattern was 
INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE, which is still valid and still common. But MERGE is now
the "I know modern Postgres" answer when the requirement involves multiple branching cases (insert-
this, update-that, delete-the-third).
Sample question phrasing:
"Given a staging table of customer updates and a target customer table, write a single statement
that inserts new customers, updates existing customers if the data has changed, and marks
customers as inactive if they are present in the target but absent from staging."
The expected 2026 answer is a MERGE statement with three WHEN clauses.
2. Logical replication improvements in Postgres 16
Postgres 16 added the ability to perform logical replication from standby servers, and various filter
improvements. Interview-relevant because data engineers are now expected to know whether their CDC
pipeline reads from primary or replica.
Sample question phrasing:
"If your downstream CDC consumer needs to be isolated from the primary write load, what
changed in Postgres 16 that lets you do this cleanly?"
Expected answer: logical replication from standbys.
3. JSON path operators @? and @@
Postgres added SQL/JSON path support a few releases back, and adoption has caught up. The @?
operator tests path existence; @@ tests a path predicate. These are showing up in interview rounds
where the candidate has Postgres on their CV.

A small but interview-relevant addition: ANY_VALUE returns an arbitrary value from a group. Useful when

Expected answer: ANY_VALUE(name).

Not new, but newly-asked in 2026. The operator handles NULL correctly where = does not. Interview

Stored generated columns shipped in Postgres 12 but adoption is now mainstream and interviewers

Sample question phrasing:
"Given a transactions table with amount and currency, you want a permanently-stored

Postgres partitioning continues to mature. Default partitions, list partition pruning, and partition-wise

Expected answer: filter on the partition key directly with literal or stable expressions; avoid functions on
the partition key that prevent pruning.

MySQL 8.x shifts
MySQL 8 finally added window functions and CTEs, and the interview market has caught up. If you
