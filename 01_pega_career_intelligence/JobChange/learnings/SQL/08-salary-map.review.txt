Data-Role Salary Map — India, 2026
Anchor your number. Negotiate from data, not gut feel.
The ranges in this file are aggregated from publicly reported numbers (Levels.fyi India, AmbitionBox,
recent offer screenshots circulating in developer communities and Telegram groups) plus pattern-
matching from candidate reports we have seen over the last 12 months. Use them as anchoring, not
gospel. Your number depends on your specific company, team, the niche skill you bring, your current
CTC, the urgency of the hiring need, and frankly the mood of the hiring manager that quarter. Treat this
as a sanity check — if your number is in this map's middle, you're priced fairly; if you're 30% below
midpoint, you should be having a different conversation with your manager or with the market.
How to read this map
All numbers are total CTC in LPA (Lakhs Per Annum) — that's base + variable + estimated ESOP/stock
value at offer, not just base. India compensation is almost always quoted as CTC in offer letters, so we
match that convention.
Variable pay assumption per tier: - Service MNCs: variable is 5–10% of CTC, almost always paid in full
unless the company missed quarterly targets. - Product companies: variable is 10–20% of CTC, payout
linked to individual rating + company performance. Plan for 80–90% in a normal year. - GCCs: variable is
10–15%, usually paid out close to 100% — these orgs treat variable as deferred fixed. - Startups:
variable is small (0–10%); the rest is ESOP. ESOPs in Indian startups are often valued at zero on exit —
treat them as bonus optionality, not real pay.
When a range says "8–14 LPA", read it as: 25th percentile around 8, median around 11, 75th percentile
around 14. Hitting the top of the range requires either a niche skill premium (see Section 5) or strong
track record (Section 6).

Backend Developer (SQL-heavy) — A Java / Python / Node / Go engineer who writes SQL daily for
application workloads. Owns transactional schemas, query performance for product features, ORM
debugging, and the occasional ETL written in application code. Not a data engineer — their primary
deliverable is the API or the service, with SQL as a load-bearing tool.
Data Analyst — SQL plus a reporting tool (Tableau / Looker / PowerBI / Metabase), business-facing.
Spends the day answering questions from product, marketing, ops, and finance. Builds dashboards,
runs ad-hoc cohort analyses, writes weekly business reviews. Often the first data hire at a startup. Career
path splits later into analytics engineering, data science, or product analytics.
Analytics Engineer — The role that exists because data analysts kept writing the same CTEs and
dashboards kept breaking. Lives in dbt on top of Snowflake / BigQuery / Redshift. Owns the modeling
layer — staging, marts, metrics definitions. Business-facing but more rigorous than a pure analyst.
Newish role in India (~3 years old as a distinct title), still scarce, still pays a premium.
Data Engineer — Pipelines. Airflow / Dagster / Prefect for orchestration; Spark / Flink / dbt for
transformation; Snowflake / Databricks / BigQuery as the warehouse; Kafka / Kinesis if streaming. Infra-
facing role — your customer is the data scientist, the analyst, the ML engineer. You worry about late-
arriving data, backfills, schema drift, and cost.
BI Engineer / BI Developer — Heavy reporting stack ownership: PowerBI / Tableau / Looker / Qlik /
SSRS. The dashboard is the deliverable. In larger enterprises this is a distinct career track; in startups it
often blurs into analytics engineering or data analyst. Premium when paired with strong SQL and
semantic modeling skills (LookML, DAX, Tableau calculated fields at scale).
DBA (Database Administrator) — Postgres / Oracle / SQL Server / MySQL operations. Performance
tuning, query plan reviews, index strategy, backup / restore, replication topology, HA, failover drills,
version upgrades. A shrinking-but-still-paid role at large enterprises (banks, BFSI, telecom) and a quietly
well-paid role at product companies that run their own databases instead of fully managed services.

What pushes you to the top of your range
Hiring managers fight harder for candidates who tick these. Each one is worth roughly 5–10% in your
number.
Owned a P0 system end-to-end — a revenue-critical pipeline, a customer-facing dashboard, the
order-flow query that runs on every checkout. Not "contributed to". Owned: on-call, debug at 2 AM,
design doc with your name on it.
Snowflake / BigQuery / Redshift cost optimization with hard numbers — "I cut our warehouse
spend from $14K/month to $6K/month by reclustering three tables and rewriting four cron queries."
Specific dollar amounts beat vague claims every time.
Mentor / lead a small team without the title — onboarded juniors, ran the data team standup,
owned the on-call rotation. Recruiters can verify this in references.
Ran a migration — Oracle to Postgres, on-prem warehouse to Snowflake, MySQL sharding, a
Hadoop deprecation. Migrations require breadth, patience, and political capital. They pay.
Conference talk, OSS contribution, or written content showing domain depth — even a
moderate blog with 4 well-researched posts beats a quiet LinkedIn. Signal of taste.
Hired a junior who stayed 1+ years — proves you can interview, onboard, and retain. Most
engineers can't do all three.
Internal mobility track record — switched teams 1–2 times, took on a new domain, moved from
analyst to analytics engineer or backend to data eng. Signals adaptability.
You can explain trade-offs out loud — "We picked Snowflake over BigQuery because our team
already knew SQL Server semantics and our workload was bursty, not steady-state." Specificity
wins interviews.
Quantifiable impact in numbers your CTO would care about — latency reduced from 800 ms to
120 ms, weekly active users up 14%, ETL runtime from 6 hours to 40 minutes, an audit failure
turned into a clean report.
A track record of saying "we shouldn't build this" — pushed back on a feature, prevented an
over-engineered solution, killed a project. Senior signal.
What pushes you to the bottom of your range
Honest list. Recruiters and hiring managers see these patterns and adjust offers down.
Stayed in the same role for 5+ years with no scope expansion — same team, same systems, no
promotion, no lateral. Suggests stagnation.
No production ownership of any system — "I worked on it" doesn't survive a 30-minute deep
dive. If the interviewer can't ask you about an incident you debugged at 11 PM, you don't own
anything.

Negotiation thresholds — when to push, when to accept
Always negotiate: Any offer that lands >10% below the midpoint of your role + experience + company-
tier range in this map. Even a polite single email pushing for 8–12% more works 50% of the time. The
downside is zero; recruiters expect a counter.
Worth pushing on: - Signing bonus — companies bend on this more than base because it doesn't
recur in next year's salary budget. Especially true for product cos and GCCs. - Notice-period buyout —
if you're servicing 60–90 days notice at your current company, ask the new employer to cover the
buyout. They almost always will up to ₹2–5L. - Variable-pay percentage — push for a higher base and
lower variable. Variable is a real risk: 80–90% payout in normal years means 0–60% payout in bad years.
- Joining date flexibility — if you can join in 30 days, that itself is leverage. Use it. - ESOP refresh
cadence at product cos — some give refresh grants every 2 years, some only at promotion. Ask.
Hard to move: - Equity refresh in Service MNCs — rare. Don't waste leverage here. - Title — often
locked to internal job bands. You can push from "Senior Data Engineer" to "Lead Data Engineer" maybe
once in a negotiation cycle; never twice. - Location / remote — varies dramatically by company. Ask in
the first call, not at offer stage. - Probation terms — almost no company will waive a 6-month probation
just because you asked.
Walk-away signals: - Offer is <70% of your researched range AND there is no credible 12-month path to
growth. - Vague answers on raise cycle ("we review when we can", "depends on company
performance"). - "We'll discuss compensation after probation" — never accept this. Discuss it now or
walk. - Recruiter ghosts you for 2+ weeks after offer discussion — signals internal chaos. - Hiring
manager refuses to discuss the team's last 3 attrition cases.

The "joining bonus" play
Joining bonuses are often the easiest lever to move because the money comes from a different budget
line — talent acquisition, not the salary budget. The new manager doesn't have to defend it as a
recurring cost.
Typical ranges (one-time, paid in 1–2 instalments, almost always with a claw-back clause if you exit
within 12–24 months):
Service MNC: ₹50K–₹3L. Smaller, but doable. Push when switching from a competitor that they
want you out of.
Product Company: ₹2L–₹15L. Standard, especially for niche skills (Snowflake, dbt, real-time data).
GCC of a US firm: ₹3L–₹20L. The highest, especially if they're replacing an attrition or filling a long-
vacant slot. AmEx, JPMC, Goldman go up to ₹25L for hard-to-find senior data engineers.
Indian Startup (Series A/B): ₹2L–₹8L. Some swap base for joining bonus to keep cap-table optics
clean; treat as compressed base.
Two practical asks to layer on:
"Can I get the joining bonus in two parts — half at joining, half at the 12-month mark — so the claw-
back stays simple?" Most recruiters say yes; this de-risks both sides.
"Can the notice-period buyout be paid directly to my current employer's invoice, not as a
reimbursement?" Saves you the temporary cash crunch.
Always read the claw-back clause carefully. Standard is full repayment if you exit before 12 months, 50%
if you exit between 12–24 months. Anything stricter than this — push back.
YoY salary inflation note
A short honest framing: 2022–2023 was an anomaly. Indian product companies and GCCs ran a one-
time correction, with 30–50% YoY jumps becoming common for mid-level data and backend engineers
— driven by post-COVID hiring, the brief crypto/Web3 cash surge, and inflated US startup funding
flowing into India. That window closed in mid-2023.
2024 and 2025 normalized to 10–15% YoY raises for internal promotions and roughly 25–40% jumps
when switching jobs. 2026 is tracking similarly — early data from Q1 hiring suggests another normalized
year with no broad correction in sight.
Two pockets are still seeing scarcity premiums in 2026: data engineers with production Snowflake + dbt
+ streaming exposure, and analytics engineers with strong LookML / semantic-modeling experience.
Both groups are still seeing 35–50% job-switch jumps. Pure backend SQL roles and traditional DBA roles
are flat-to-modest.

One-line salary check
If your current CTC is 30%+ below the midpoint for your role / experience / company-tier in this map,
you are probably underpaid. Either fix that conversation internally in the next 6 months or start
interviewing.
If you are 20%+ above the top end of your band, you're doing something most people in the market
can't do — protect that, document it, and don't switch for less than a 50% jump. Anything less and
you're trading certainty for a story the new company hasn't earned the right to tell yet.
